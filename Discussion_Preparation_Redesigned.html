Case Discussion

Discussion Preparation

Questions to guide your analysis and prepare you for class discussion

For each assigned case, come to class prepared to take a position, support it with case evidence, and respond to the questions below. Use these questions to organize your individual preparation and pre-class discussion with classmates.

Annie’s Growing Organically • Sept. 2

  1. What market segment or segments should Annie’s target for the frozen pizza launch? What makes the segment(s) attractive?
  2. If the frozen pizza launch succeeds, what other new products should Annie’s consider launching? What product categories should it focus on?
  3. What pricing strategy should Annie’s pursue for the frozen pizza launch? Should it change its current pricing strategy? What pricing issues factor into the decision?
  4. Should Annie’s launch frozen pizza in the natural foods channel, mass merchandiser channel, or traditional grocery store channel? Which offers the greatest chance of a successful launch? What channel issues factor into the decision?
  5. Will Annie’s current marketing communications strategy work for the frozen pizza launch, or will changes be necessary?

Saxonville Sausage • Sept. 9

  1. What is the company’s current situation?
  2. Was the company’s marketing research methodology sound? How did each step in the research sequence serve the purpose of developing a positioning?
  3. Describe a positioning “ladder” for each of the two finalist Italian sausage positionings.
  4. Which of the two final alternatives do you recommend and why? What tactics (4Ps) would be consistent with your recommendation?

Hunley: Casting for Growth • Sept. 23

Decision focus: Be prepared to make a clear go/no-go recommendation for each opportunity and defend the associated marketing plan.
  1. Should Hunley make and sell the Grand Bassarra (GB) Titaluk River fly rod? Why or why not? If yes, recommend a marketing plan.
  2. Should Hunley make and sell a fly rod through Walmart? Why or why not? If yes, recommend a marketing plan.

Peak Sealing Technologies • Sept. 30

  1. What makes Peak Sealing Technologies (PST) a successful company? What core competencies and organizational values allow the company to stand out?
  2. Should PST launch an economy tape? What are the pros and cons of introducing one?
  3. Assume PST will launch an economy tape. What issues or problems must be resolved to ensure a successful launch? Propose an action plan using the marketing 4Ps that offers the best chance of overcoming these issues and problems.

Chase Sapphire: Creating a Millennial Sensation • Oct. 7

  1. Is the Chase Sapphire Reserve Card good for JPMorgan Chase (JPMC)? Is it good for the Sapphire family of brands? Why or why not?
  2. What drove the Sapphire Reserve Card’s success at acquiring customers? Do you think this success will continue? Why or why not? Should the card’s customer acquisition strategy change?
  3. Given the customer prototypes below, how can the Chase Sapphire team best design its product and brand to attract the right customers? Has it done so successfully with the Chase Sapphire Reserve? Why or why not?
Customer prototype assumptions
  • Transactors / Revolvers: Annual card spend = $16,000; credit balance carried = $0 for transactors and 50% of annual spend for revolvers; average interest rate = 21%; customers renew at the $450 annual fee.
  • Dormants: Annual card spend is just enough to earn the sign-on bonus; credit balance carried = $0; customers do not renew at the $450 annual fee.
  1. At the time of the case, should competing card providers respond to the Chase Sapphire Reserve? If yes, how? How might Chase prepare for potential competitive responses?
  2. Will Chase succeed at retaining Chase Sapphire Reserve customers in the years to come? Why? What could it do to maintain or increase customer retention?
  3. Evaluate the Chase Sapphire brand and product portfolio: no-fee Chase Sapphire, Chase Sapphire Preferred, and Chase Sapphire Reserve. What are its strengths? What problems might arise? Are three cards enough or too many? What changes, if any, would you recommend?

Curled Metals Inc. • Oct. 21

  1. What factors will affect a buyer’s willingness-to-pay (WTP) for Curled Metals’ “revolutionary” new cushion pad?
  2. What should a customer be willing to pay for one of its new 11.5-inch pads?
  3. What price should Curled Metals charge for one of its new 11.5-inch pads? Why?
  4. Assume the new pad is the “way of the future” and eventually all cushion-pad users switch to it. How large is the potential market in number of pads? Roughly estimate how many pads the entire market will buy each year.

Springfield Nor’easters • Oct. 28

  1. What are the key findings of the research survey? What do they reveal about the prospective customer profile, pricing, and single-ticket versus season-ticket packages?
  2. What should the Nor’easters take into account in establishing a pricing policy?
  3. Design a ticket-pricing plan for the Nor’easters’ first season. Be specific. Should Buckingham offer more than one type of season package? How, if at all, should ticket pricing vary by package type?
  4. Using your pricing plan and Buckingham’s assumptions about concession sales, will the team reach breakeven in the first year? If not, what options does Buckingham have to reach his target?

Purple Innovation • Nov. 4

  1. What is driving the increase in the number of direct-to-consumer mattress companies?
  2. How would you summarize Purple’s value proposition? What benefits does it promise that other companies do not?
  3. Do bricks-and-mortar mattress retailers offer consumers value they might not otherwise obtain from online mattress vendors? Explain.
  4. Play the role of Purple’s chief marketing officer and propose a marketing communications plan aimed at building offline mattress sales at bricks-and-mortar stores. Focus specifically on a digital advertising campaign: What segment will you target? What message or theme will you use? What imagery should the ads include? Where should the ads appear? How will you determine whether the campaign is working?

Clique Pens • Nov. 18

  1. How do the aims of market development funds differ from discounts to the trade?
  2. Whose needs should concern Clique the most: retailers or consumers? Why?
  3. Should Clique establish market development funds as a marketing initiative, or primarily as a sales tool for gaining shelf space? What are the pros and cons of each approach?

Promoting Land & Nature Jerky • Nov. 25

  1. Explain why each of the following factors must be evaluated before deciding whether to launch a national sales promotion: cannibalization, brand-equity erosion, forward-buying, pass-through, and consumer stockpiling.
  2. Should Sanchez run a national sales promotion? Why or why not? If yes, which product should receive the promotional funds: (a) Dinardo’s™ 32-ounce packages, (b) Dinardo’s™ 16-ounce packages, or (c) Natural Meals™?
Preparation standard: Arrive ready to make a recommendation—not merely summarize the case. Strong preparation uses specific case evidence, anticipates competing arguments, and identifies the tradeoffs behind the decision.
