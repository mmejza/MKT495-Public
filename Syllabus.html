Course Description & Objectives

The goal of this course is to build your ability to think, speak, and write critically, creatively, and convincingly about marketing strategies that deliver and sustain value to customers and meet company objectives for commercial success. In short, the aim is to enable you to begin practicing the marketing discipline.

By completing the course, you should be able to:

  1. Assess the potential of markets and effectively select target segments.
  2. Develop useful positioning to help a firm’s offering stand out against the competition.
  3. Develop marketing tactics to achieve a positioning in a target market.

Course Delivery & Learning Modes

1. Online Synchronous Class Sessions

This course consists of regular class sessions held in Zoom. Attendance and participation in each class session is required. If you were part of a business’s marketing team, you would be expected to attend and participate in all team meetings. The same expectation holds for this course.

2. Case Study Learning

Course objectives will be achieved through the analysis, evaluation, and discussion of case studies. Since marketing strategy decisions involve finding solutions to marketing problems, simulating such situations nurtures the skills needed to identify and implement those solutions. Business case studies provide a platform on which quantitative and qualitative skills may be developed and exercised.

We will read and discuss a wide range of marketing strategy cases that present unique, complex, and sometimes confusing circumstances. Our challenge will be to draw lessons or takeaways from each case that can be generalized across situations.

To build a foundation for advanced case discussion, the course materials include five readings from the Harvard Business School Core Curriculum (HBS CC). Your weekly study should center first on comprehending the assigned HBS CC reading and, second, on preparing arguments and answers to the discussion questions for that week’s case.

3. Quizzes

Five quizzes will assess comprehension and application of concepts and frameworks discussed in the HBS CC readings. Quizzes will consist of 10–15 multiple-choice questions and will be administered using Respondus LockDown Browser at the start of the corresponding class session.

4. Breakout Group & Class Discussions

Breakout group exercises and class discussions of case studies will be this course’s primary learning activity. Case study learning is participant-centered: students generate the greatest activity and assume responsibility for advancing the class’s collective understanding.

It is critical that you commit to owning class discussion. You can best do this by being open to new ideas, sharing your ideas, listening carefully, and commenting constructively on the ideas expressed by classmates. Enthusiasm, informed opinion, counter-intuitive insight, and healthy skepticism are welcome; cynicism is not.

The instructor will guide discussion, challenge arguments, provoke debate, and summarize learning. However, students must accept ownership of the case discussions. The instructor is the only person in the course who does not need to make a decision about the case.

Preparation expectation. For each case, prepare a recommendation for the main decision maker and be ready to explain its rationale and details. Discussion questions for each case are posted in WebCampus.

The best preparation system involves individual and group study, in that order. Individual study should come first so that you master the facts and issues and formulate your own recommendations. Then meet with classmates to test arguments and exchange viewpoints. You are encouraged to form and participate in study groups during the semester.

Case-information restriction. During preparation, you are forbidden from consulting outside sources of information about the company or situation discussed in the case. Outside sources include library materials, the Internet, newspapers, trade publications, and people outside the class who are familiar with the case or situation. The exceptions are marketing textbooks or articles from previous courses that explain conceptual frameworks for marketing decision-making.

5. Case Takeaways

Eight case-study “takeaway” assignments will reinforce insights from case discussions and relevant marketing-management concepts and frameworks. You will identify and clearly write lessons you internalize after reading and discussing a case. Takeaways may be (1) general lessons relevant to marketing managers across industries or (2) lessons relevant to a specific company’s past, present, or future situation. Each assignment will remain available for one week after the class in which the case is discussed. Case takeaway assignments carry a 5% grading weight.

6. Team Case Essays

Two written team case-decision essays will be used to hone your ability to develop persuasive written arguments about marketing-strategy decisions.

Team Essay Due Date Grade Weight
“Hunley: Casting for Growth” Sept. 23 10%
“Clique Pens . . .” Nov. 18 10%

For each essay, the instructor will assign teams of 3–4 students. Within two weeks after submitting each team essay, each student must submit a completed peer evaluation. Peer evaluations may be used to adjust an individual’s team essay score when corroborative evidence of unusually high or low effort arises.

Teams should deliver integrated essays with one narrative voice that incorporates each member’s input. Dividing the essay into independently written sections and simply compiling them typically weakens the final product.

Required essay structure
  1. Position Statement
  2. Decision Options
  3. Decision Criteria
  4. Proof of Recommended Option
  5. Critique of Rejected Options
  6. Disadvantages of Recommended Option
  7. Action Plan

Quality / Impact of Content 75

  • Double line spacing, page numbers in the footer, 1-inch margins, and 12-point font.
  • Professional title page; title repeated in the page header.
  • Six-page or 1,500-word limit for written text. Exhibits, graphs, diagrams, and similar supporting materials are not subject to that limit.

If the course becomes difficult, it is your responsibility to tell the instructor so that help may be given. Assistance is more apt to take the form of direction than the provision of solutions.
