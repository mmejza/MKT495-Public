Case Decision Essay Example

Whistler Corporation: a partially completed model of the seven-part structure

This example illustrates how the required sections work together. It is intentionally incomplete: highlighted coaching notes identify places where a writer must add case evidence or extend the analysis.

Use it as a structural model, not as a template for copying language.

Decision: How should Whistler respond to the cost and competitiveness problems in its domestic radar-detector manufacturing operations?

1. Recommendation Statement

The Chief Operating Officer should move all domestic production offshore. The company is losing $6 million a year and cannot sustain these losses and remain in business.

Complete the opening: Preview the other criteria-based reasons that will be developed in the proof.

2. Decision Options

  1. Reengineer domestic production.
  2. Move all production offshore.
  3. Transfer only low-end products offshore.

3. Decision Criteria

CriterionMeaning in This Decision
Strategic importanceDetermine the strategic importance of radar detectors to Whistler.
Manufacturing fitSelect the manufacturing solution best suited to that strategic importance.

4. Proof of the Recommended Option

The second option best satisfies both criteria. Whistler’s long-term competitive advantage resides in design and engineering. Moving all production offshore will enable the company to concentrate on its core strengths and free resources for expanded new-product development.

Evidence needed: Introduce specific case facts that establish design and engineering as Whistler’s sources of advantage.

Moreover, demand, prices, and profitability are declining, and the case provides no reason to expect a reversal. Competition is increasingly based on price, which favors the company with the lowest costs. Whistler is clearly not winning that contest, and most competitors have already moved production offshore.

Evidence needed: Support the claims about market decline, price competition, and competitors’ manufacturing choices. Add any other criteria-based reasons for the recommendation.

Conclusion: Whistler should follow the direction of its competitors and pursue the lowest sustainable unit cost through outsourcing.

5. Critique of Rejected Options

Rejected Option: Transfer Only Low-End Products Offshore

The COO should eliminate this option because it preserves Whistler’s flawed manufacturing process. The problems in Whistler’s plants resulted from a long series of decisions. When the radar-detector market expanded in the late 1980s, Whistler introduced many products and added capacity quickly without redesigning manufacturing for the increased volume and complexity.

Evidence needed: Add other case evidence showing why partial outsourcing fails the criteria.

Whistler’s costs exceed offshore plants’ costs by 33 percent. Moving only additional volume to Korea would narrow the difference slightly while leaving the domestic manufacturing problems unresolved.

Rejected Option: Reengineer Domestic Production

Reengineering could improve financial results if MPL is implemented and the Fitchburg plant is closed. However, the MPL results may not scale across the company. Even with MPL’s benefits, Whistler’s domestic unit cost remains 11 percent higher than the cost of Asian vendors.

Evidence needed: Add other case evidence showing why domestic reengineering does not adequately satisfy the criteria.

Because it is uncertain that the domestic plants can become cost competitive with offshore vendors, reengineering is not the best option.

6. Disadvantages of the Recommended Option

Outsourcing entrusts an entire function to another organization. An unreliable partner could create a crisis worse than the current one. The greatest disadvantage, however, is the closing of domestic plants and the resulting human cost. Layoffs will be traumatic for affected workers and could reduce morale, productivity, and commitment among remaining employees.

Thorough due diligence can reduce partnership risk. Whistler’s current outsourcing partner is a leading candidate because the firms have a good relationship and the vendor knows Whistler’s products. The effects of plant closings cannot be eliminated, but they can be contained. Whistler should provide transition assistance and retraining where possible, explain the reasons for the closures honestly, and show how the decision improves the company’s prospects for survival and preserving remaining jobs.

7. Action Plan

Goal: Begin transferring production to a qualified partner within one year and complete the transition within two years.

Short-Term Actions

  • Build senior-management consensus on the decision, schedule, and process; form a cross-functional transition team.
  • Develop worker-assistance and communication plans before announcing the decision.
  • Evaluate the current Korean partner and other candidates using quality, delivery performance, and working-relationship criteria.
  • Select a partner, agree on schedules and controls, and pilot the transfer with one product.

Medium-Term Actions

  • Begin outplacement services and improve them using feedback from the first round.
  • Complete the transfer and establish close technical and project coordination with the partner.

Long-Term Action

  • Manage the supplier as a strategic partner and invest in a healthy long-term relationship.

What to notice: The recommendation, criteria, proof, rejection of alternatives, risks, and implementation steps all address the same decision. That alignment—not the amount of case summary—is what makes the essay persuasive.
