Clique Pens: Put Your Recommendation to the Test

Can your team's recommendation increase profits, satisfy retailers, and survive a competitive response?

Assume your team just presented its recommendation to Elise Ferguson. She is willing to consider it, but wants evidence that it will work and asked your team to stress-test the recommendation by addressing its weaknesses, and, if necessary, modify the recommendation.

Task 1 — Find the Weak Points (15 minutes)

Identify what could prevent your recommendation from succeeding.

  1. What are the two most important assumptions behind your recommendation?
  2. Which assumption is least supported by evidence in the case?
  3. Who has the strongest reason to oppose your recommendation—retailers, consumers, sales, or marketing?
  4. What could go wrong even if Clique implements your recommendation exactly as planned?

Conclude: Identify the single greatest vulnerability and explain how Clique could determine whether the risk is serious.

Task 2 — Make the Numbers Work (20 minutes)

Ferguson wants Clique to increase gross profit by 4%. Your recommendation must have a plausible path to that objective. Use the 2013 estimates from Exhibit 1:

Measure Value
Sales $187 million
Gross profit $67 million
Reported gross profit margin 36%
Gross profit target (+4%) $69.68 million
  1. What gross profit margin do you expect your recommendation to produce? Explain your assumption.
  2. At that margin, how much revenue must Clique generate to meet Ferguson's target?
  3. If sales fall short of your expectation by 5%, will your strategy still meet the target?
  4. What evidence in the case supports your revenue and margin expectations?
Calculation reminder. Gross profit = Revenue × Gross profit margin. Use $67 million as the starting gross profit for Ferguson's 4% growth target. 

Conclude: State the financial conditions under which your recommendation succeeds and the point at which it fails.

Task 3 — Win Retailer Support (20 minutes)

Your recommendation may benefit Clique, but retailers do not necessarily share the Clique's objectives. Choose one of the three retailers below and design an agreement it might realistically accept.

Retailer What matters to its buyer
Staples Realized gross margin
Walmart Cost concessions and merchandising programs
Walgreens Favorable payment terms and other buyer incentives
  1. What change are you asking the retailer to accept?
  2. What would the retailer gain, and what might it lose?
  3. What specific incentive or support would Clique offer?
  4. What measurable commitment would Clique require in return?
  5. At what point would the agreement become too costly for Clique?

Conclude: Describe the agreement you would propose, including what each party gives and receives.

Task 4 — Respond to a Competitive Threat (20 minutes)

Just as Clique prepares to implement your strategy, a competitor takes action.

New development — Hypothetical scenario. A competing pen manufacturer offers one of Clique's major retailers an additional 8% wholesale discount in exchange for preferred shelf placement. The retailer gives Clique one week to respond.
  1. Should Clique match the discount, counter with a different offer, or risk losing shelf space?
  2. How would your response affect Clique's gross profit?
  3. Would your decision change if the retailer represented 5% rather than 20% of Clique's sales?
  4. Does this development reveal a weakness in your original recommendation?

Conclude: Decide how Clique should respond and explain what it is willing to sacrifice to protect its longer-term position.

Final Recommendation — Ferguson's Decision (15 minutes)

Prepare a concise executive recommendation for the team wrap-up and class discussion. Cover:

  1. Your original recommendation and its most important weakness.
  2. The financial conditions required for success.
  3. Your proposed retailer agreement.
  4. Your response to the competitive threat.
  5. Your final decision: Keep, modify, or abandon your original recommendation?
