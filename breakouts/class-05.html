AI can improve apparent answer quality while masking what you have not learned. A polished answer does not demonstrate understanding. Your marketing knowledge is what lets you recognize sound reasoning, missing evidence, or a plausible but flawed claim.

01 Make the case recommendation5–18 minutes • Team thinking • No AI

  1. Decision: State the decision Hunley faces and the viable alternatives.
  2. Criteria: Choose the criteria that should govern the decision. Identify the decisive one.
  3. Recommendation: Choose a course of action and support it with specific case evidence.
  4. Tradeoff: Acknowledge its disadvantages; explain why you rejected the strongest alternative.

The One-Reason Test: If you could give only one reason to accept your recommendation, what would it be? Build a case argument you can deliver within five minutes.

02 Design both launch strategies18–35 minutes • Team thinking • No AI

Develop both opportunities, even if your case recommendation favors one. Keep entries concise and distinguish case evidence from assumptions.

OPPORTUNITY A • $800 RETAIL

1. Segment: Identify meaningful differences in needs, benefits sought, or buying behavior.

3. Position: State the benefit, competitive alternative, and reason to believe at $800.

4. Execution: Specify the primary message, proof points, print-media choices, retail outlets, and spending that follow from STP.

OPPORTUNITY B • $99 RETAIL

Walmart entry rod

2. Target: Choose a specific segment. How does it differ from your $800 target?

3. Position: State the benefit, competitive alternative, and why the offer is credible at $99.

4. Execution: Specify message, proof, media—including print support—Walmart execution, and spending.

Two plans. Two separate $1,000,000 budgets.

For each plan, allocate across print, cooperative advertising, sponsorships, point-of-sale, search, and any justified additional activities. Explain how spending supports the target and position. Check the workbook totals: each budget must equal $1,000,000.

Positioning scaffold + consistency check

For [target], [product] is [competitive frame] that delivers [distinctive benefit], because [reason to believe].

Check: If someone saw only your message, channels, and spending, could they infer your target and positioning?

03 AI Stress Test35–45 minutes • Only after your own reasoning is recorded

Use AI as a critic, not your strategist. Give it your team’s recorded recommendation, two plans, and reasoning. Ask for weaknesses in segmentation, target selection, positioning, execution fit, and assumptions. Preserve your pre-AI baseline.

Open the AI critique prompt
Our team recommends [decision] because [criterion and case evidence]. Our $800 plan is [segment, target, position, execution, budget, rationale]. Our $99 Walmart plan is [same elements]. Our weakest assumption is [assumption]. Identify the three weakest assumptions or inconsistencies, including conflicts between the two plans. Separate claims supported by the information we supplied from issues requiring verification. Ask challenging questions. Do not solve the case or create a replacement strategy.

Do not ask AI to choose the recommendation or generate either launch plan. Save the prompt and key criticisms in your team notes. If AI is unavailable, have an analyst conduct the same critique and identify it as a peer stress test.

04 Make the team judgment45–55 minutes • Evaluate the critique; finalize your choices

For each of the three main challenges, record the criticism → your judgment → supporting evidence or reasoning → any change.

ACCEPT — The criticism identifies something we missed. Explain what and its implication.

REJECT — The criticism is wrong or inappropriate. Explain why using case evidence or marketing reasoning.

REVISE — The criticism exposes a weakness; our team develops the response. Record what changed and why.

The Ownership Test

Understand: Can we explain the reasoning without AI?

Defend: Can any team member respond to a challenge?
Evaluate: Can we judge whether the marketing reasoning is sound?

Would we notice? Could we spot a plausible but flawed recommendation?

If you cannot pass this test, revisit the reasoning before adopting it. Verify AI claims against the case; label unresolved assumptions. Finalize both plans and check both budgets.

05 Collision test + prepare to defend55–65 minutes • Return to the main Zoom room ready

STP Collision Test

Compare both plans side by side in your workbook: segment, target, position, reason to believe, and execution.

  • What must change? Name the most important difference in target and positioning.
  • What must remain coherent? What must hold true for both offerings to coexist under Klamath?
  • Where do they collide? Identify how one strategy could undermine the other.
  • Make the call: Is the tension manageable? Explain how—or why not.

Prepare your report-out

Lead with an argument. Be ready to present:

  1. Your recommendation, decisive criterion, and strongest evidence.
  2. Each product’s target and position, and the most important STP contrast.
  3. The risk requiring the most active management and your response.
  4. Your weakest assumption and what evidence would change your mind.
  5. One AI criticism you accepted, rejected, or used to revise your plan—and why.

Rehearse: Have a member other than the presenter defend a choice without AI. Save the completed workbook and keep it ready to share.

Back in the main room: present, challenge, defend

Every team is also a challenge team. Your instructor will assign your team another team’s presentation to question. Listen for an assumption, STP choice, or mismatch between strategy and execution that deserves closer examination. Work together to prepare one substantive question; your Challenge Lead asks it before the discussion opens to the class.

When your team presents, your assigned challenge team gets the first question. Any member of your team may respond. The goal is to test and strengthen the reasoning, not to catch anyone out.

Each student: Contribute at least two substantive Zoom chat comments during other teams’ report-outs. Label them with one of these starters and be ready to unmute:

  • QUESTION: Challenge an assumption or decision.
  • CONTRAST: Explain how your team’s reasoning differed.
  • BUILD: Extend or strengthen the argument.
  • AI CHECK: Ask the team to explain why it accepted or rejected an AI criticism. What case evidence or marketing principles support that judgment?
