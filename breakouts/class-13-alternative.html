MKT 495 • Class 13 Breakout Activity

Clique Pens: Challenge, Defend, and Refine Your Decision

Is your original recommendation still the best choice—and can Clique make it work?

Your team has already summarized its recommendation to Elise Ferguson. She finds parts of it persuasive, but is not ready to approve it. Compare your proposal with its strongest alternative, test the financial assumptions, and work through retailer resistance. You may reaffirm, modify, or replace your original recommendation—but explain why.

Working plan: Task 1: 15 minutes • Task 2: 20 minutes • Task 3: 15 minutes • Task 4: 20 minutes • Task 5: 10 minutes • Final discussion: 10 minutes.

Team output: One executive decision summary supported by your financial comparison, decisive assumption, and retailer proposal. Record your answers as you work. Be ready to explain what changed from your original recommendation and why.

Task 1 — Reconsider the Decision (15 minutes)

Begin with the recommendation your team presented earlier. Do not assume it is correct simply because you have already defended it. Identify the strongest credible alternative Ferguson could choose.

  1. State your original recommendation in one or two sentences. What is the mechanism by which it should improve Clique’s profitability?
  2. Describe the strongest alternative. Be specific about how trade allowances, market development funds (MDF), consumer promotions, or decision authority would change.
  3. Make the best case against your original recommendation. What would a thoughtful manager who disagrees with you argue?
  4. Identify the most consequential tradeoff between the two approaches. Who gains, who loses, and why?

Conclude: State the main reason your original recommendation might still be preferable—and the strongest reason to doubt it.

Task 2 — Test the Economics (20 minutes)

Ferguson wants Clique to increase gross profit by 4%. Compare the financial implications of your original recommendation and the strongest alternative using the case’s 2013 estimates (Exhibit 1).

2013 case baselineValue
Sales$187 million
Gross profit$67 million
Reported gross profit margin36% (rounded)
Target gross profit (4% growth)$69.68 million
  1. For each approach, estimate the change in Clique’s sales and its resulting gross profit margin. Label these as assumptions, not established facts.
  2. Calculate projected sales and gross profit for each approach. Does either reach $69.68 million?
  3. Which approach appears financially stronger? How large is the difference?
  4. What evidence in the case supports or challenges your estimates? Consider Chen’s and McMillan’s competing forecasts.
Keep the calculations consistent. Projected sales = $187 million × (1 + assumed sales change). Projected gross profit = projected sales × assumed gross profit margin. Use the reported $67 million as the baseline for the 4% growth target; the case’s 36% margin is rounded and does not exactly reconcile with $67 million ÷ $187 million. Avoid counting the same promotional savings twice.

Conclude: Which approach has the stronger financial case, and what makes that conclusion uncertain?

Task 3 — Identify the Decisive Assumption (15 minutes)

A financial forecast is only as useful as the assumptions behind it. Find the one assumption most likely to reverse your preference between the two approaches.

  1. Identify that assumption and explain why it matters more than the others.
  2. Separate case facts, Chen’s or McMillan’s predictions, and your team’s assumptions. Which category does your decisive assumption belong to?
  3. Change the assumption to a plausible unfavorable value. Recalculate the affected financial result or explain precisely how the comparison changes.
  4. What would have to be true for your second-choice approach to become the better decision?

Conclude: Write a decision rule: “We would favor the alternative if _______.” State what evidence Clique would need to test that condition.

Task 4 — Confront Retailer Resistance (20 minutes)

Now consider whether the approach you currently favor can be implemented. Choose one retailer and develop an agreement it might accept. The case describes different incentives for buyers at these accounts.

RetailerBuyer incentive described in the case
StaplesRealized gross margin
WalmartWarehouse allowances, rollback promotions, and merchandising programs within a set-margin environment
WalgreensDating and payment terms
  1. What change are you asking the retailer to accept, and why might its buyer resist?
  2. What specific concession, MDF support, or other benefit would Clique provide?
  3. What measurable commitment would the retailer provide in return (for example, agreed display space or verified promotional execution)?
  4. What does this agreement cost Clique? Does it require revising your Task 2 financial assumptions?
  5. What is your walk-away condition?

Conclude: Describe the proposed exchange: what Clique gives, what the retailer commits to, and why both would agree. Explain whether this negotiation strengthens or weakens your preferred strategy.

Task 5 — Make the Final Decision (10 minutes)

Ferguson wants one recommendation, not a list of possibilities. Decide whether to reaffirm, modify, or replace your original strategy.

  1. State the final recommendation and the specific actions Clique should take.
  2. Explain why it is preferable to the strongest alternative, using your Task 2 comparison.
  3. State the assumption that matters most and the condition that would cause you to change course.
  4. Explain how your retailer proposal affects the decision.
  5. Identify one initial implementation step or limited test, and what Clique should measure before expanding the strategy.

Conclude: What survived from your original recommendation, what changed, and why?

Report-Out — What Changed Your Mind? (10 minutes)

Be ready to give a concise account of your final decision, emphasizing the reasoning rather than repeating your earlier presentation:

  1. Did you reaffirm, modify, or replace the original recommendation?
  2. What was the most important insight from comparing the alternatives?
  3. Which assumption could reverse your choice?
  4. What did the retailer negotiation reveal about feasibility?

Discussion question: Did you change your recommendation because you discovered new information, or because you evaluated information you already had more carefully?

What your instructor is looking for: a credible competing alternative, calculations consistent with stated assumptions, a willingness to identify what could prove your decision wrong, a concrete retailer agreement, and a final decision supported by the analysis.
