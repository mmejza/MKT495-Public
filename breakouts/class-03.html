MKT 495 • Class 3 Breakout Activity

Saxonville Sausage

Research design, insight development, positioning, and AI-assisted analysis

Trace how research becomes a positioning decision. Use case evidence, distinguish findings from recommendations, and use AI as an analytical resource—not as a substitute for your group’s judgment.

Download the report-out workbook

How We Will Work Today

This is a live Zoom working session. During breakout groups and the class report-out:

  • Keep your camera on and remain visibly engaged with your group. You may be reading the case, examining exhibits, working in the workbook, or interacting with AI; continuous eye contact with the camera is not expected.
  • AI use is permitted and encouraged except in Activity 3. Where permitted, your group may use chatbots or agents to generate alternatives, critique ideas, test assumptions, or identify weaknesses. You must build both competing positioning plans in Activity 3 without AI assistance.
  • AI does not make the decision for your group. Your group is responsible for determining what to accept, reject, or revise.
  • Every group member is accountable for the analysis. The instructor may ask any member—not only the spokesperson or person operating the chatbot—to explain, challenge, or defend the group’s reasoning.
  • If circumstances require you to turn your camera off temporarily, remain actively engaged with the group.
Working principle: AI is an analyst your group supervises. You are the management team.

Activity 1 — Map the Research Process

Summarize each stage of Saxonville’s research process. For every stage, identify:

What the researchers did → Why they did it → What they learned → What decision or next step it enabled

Treat the two phases of the first research stage separately.

Use AI deliberately

You may ask AI to help reconstruct or critique the research process. Do not simply accept its summary.

Before completing this section, your group should identify one place where AI may be confusing:

  • a research activity with a finding,
  • a finding with an insight, or
  • an insight with a recommendation.

Be ready to defend: What case evidence gives your group permission to make each important inference?

Activity 2 — Design Four Focus Groups

Using the Exhibit 3 variables, design four focus groups that would help Saxonville learn something meaningfully different from each group. For each group, specify:

  • screening criteria;
  • why this group should generate useful insight;
  • what this group contributes that the other three do not.

Then evaluate the design as a whole: What important customer variation does your design capture? What important blind spot might remain?

Put AI to work—and then challenge it

One useful approach:

  1. Ask AI to propose a four-group design.
  2. Identify the weakest element of its design.
  3. Revise it.
  4. Ask AI to criticize your revised design.
  5. Decide which criticisms your group accepts and rejects.

Your final design should represent your group’s judgment, not simply the first plausible segmentation an AI produces.

Be ready to answer: Why should these four groups think or behave differently in ways that matter to Saxonville?

Activity 3 — Build Two Competing Positioning Plans

Develop complete positioning plans for Family Connection and Clever Cooking. For each, build the brand ladder from bottom to top:

Attributes → Functional benefits → Emotional benefits → Core value

Then develop:

Positioning statement → Product forms/flavors → Packaging → Merchandising → Tagline

Complete Activity 3 without AI

Do not use chatbots, agents, or other generative AI tools to build either competing positioning plan.

Your group—not AI—must develop the brand ladders, positioning statements, product and flavor recommendations, packaging, merchandising, and taglines for both Family Connection and Clever Cooking.

Use the case evidence and your group’s own reasoning to make the alternatives genuinely compete. Be prepared to explain how every element follows from the positioning.

Which position has the stronger strategic logic and stronger case evidence?

Activity 4 — Make the Decision

Select Family Connection or Clever Cooking. Before choosing, establish the criteria your group will use to evaluate the alternatives. Possible criteria might include:

customer relevance • research support • differentiation • credibility • execution potential • growth potential

Then make and defend your choice. Your group should also identify:

  • The strongest argument for the position you rejected.
  • One piece of additional evidence that could cause you to reverse your decision.

AI Judgment Check

Before leaving the breakout room, identify one substantive AI contribution that your group rejected, corrected, or substantially modified. Record:

What AI suggested → What concerned your group → What you changed → Why

This will be part of the report-out.

Report-Out

Be prepared to present:

  1. Research: One important insight about how the research process worked.
  2. Research design: The logic behind your four focus groups.
  3. Positioning: Your chosen positioning concept.
  4. Evidence: The strongest case evidence supporting your choice.
  5. AI judgment: One AI-generated idea your group rejected, corrected, or substantially improved—and why.

Everyone remains part of the discussion

The designated spokesperson may begin the report-out, but any group member may be asked to explain the reasoning, respond to another group, defend the recommendation, or identify a weakness in the group’s analysis.

Standard for today: Your group does not need to agree with the chatbot. Your group does need to understand and own everything it submits.
