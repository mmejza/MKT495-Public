Research design, insight development, and positioning

Trace how research becomes a positioning decision. Use case evidence, distinguish findings from recommendations, and record your group’s analysis in the workbook.

Download the report-out workbook

1. Map the Research Process

Summarize each of the four research steps. For every step, identify what the team did, its purpose, and the finding or decision it enabled. Treat the two phases of Step 1 separately.

Be precise: A research activity is not the same as an insight. Show the connection between method, evidence, and managerial use.

2. Design Four Focus Groups

Using the 23 variables in Exhibit 3, define four distinct focus groups. For each group:

  • Specify the relevant demographic, behavioral, and geographic screening criteria.
  • Explain why this group should produce useful insight.
  • Identify what the group contributes that the other three do not.

Then assess the design as a whole: What important variation does it capture, and what blind spot might remain?

3. Build Two Positioning Plans

Create complete plans for Family Connection and Clever Cooking. Develop each brand ladder from bottom to top:

LevelQuestion
AttributesWhat tangible features or cues support the promise?
Functional benefitsWhat does the product help the customer do?
Emotional benefitsHow should the customer feel?
Core valueWhat enduring idea anchors the position?

For each concept, also specify a positioning statement, product forms and flavors, packaging, merchandising, and tagline. Select one concept and defend it using explicit criteria.

Report-Out

Present one research-process insight, the logic of your focus-group design, your chosen positioning concept, and the strongest evidence supporting that choice.
