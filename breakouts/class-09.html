Task #1

Unlike cost-based pricing, value-based pricing requires prior knowledge about the market, especially how much customers in the market are willing to pay for a company’s product.

    • Based on information about costs and results from either the Kendrick or the Corey Construction jobs, how much should pile driving customers  be willing to pay for an 11.5 inch CMI pad?

 

Task #2

Managers should not automatically assume that what customers should be willing to pay is the price to charge for a product or service. They should also assess a product’s perceived value (and the factors that influence it) relative to its objective value when setting a price. 

    • What price should CMI charge for its 11.5 inch pads?
    • Should price be set at the level of the pad’s objective value? 
    • Should price be set lower than, equal to, or higher than the pad’s level of perceived value?
