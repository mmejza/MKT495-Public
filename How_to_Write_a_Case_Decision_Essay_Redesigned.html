Case Analysis

How to Write a Case Decision Essay

A practical framework for making, defending, and implementing a business recommendation

A strong case decision essay does more than summarize a case. It takes a clear position, explains why that position is preferable to the alternatives, supports the reasoning with evidence, and shows how the recommendation should be implemented.

What Readers Expect

Readers generally expect more discipline from written analysis than from oral discussion. In particular, they are:

  • Less tolerant of gaps in logic.
  • Less interested in the writer’s thought process.
  • More interested in the quality and clarity of the final recommendation.

What Makes a Decision Essay Persuasive?

What: State a clear recommendation.
Why: Support the recommendation with reasons and evidence.
How: Provide a practical plan for implementation.
Avoid: Restating case information unless it directly advances an argument.

What Is a Sound Argument?

A sound argument tells the reader what you claim, why the claim is valid, and what evidence supports the reasoning.

Element Meaning
WhatA claim or proposition.
WhyThe reasons the claim should be accepted as valid.
EvidenceFacts, data, calculations, exhibits, or other case information that support the reasons.

Example: Elements of an Argument

WhatThe company should increase price by 10%.
WhyAlthough market share may decline, total profit should increase.
EvidenceExhibit 1 shows a 30% contribution margin. A 10% price increase can therefore increase total profit as long as unit sales do not decline by more than 20%.

Required Structure of the Case Decision Essay

  1. Recommendation Statement
  2. Decision Options
  3. Decision Criteria
  4. Proof of the Recommended Option
  5. Critique of Rejected Options
  6. Disadvantages of the Recommended Option
  7. Action Plan

1. Recommendation Statement

  • Provides the essay with direction and purpose.
  • Should require no more than about one-third of a double-spaced page.
  • Clearly recommends one decision option.
  • Previews the criteria-based reasons supporting the recommendation.
  • Avoids unnecessary case background; assume the reader already knows the situation.

2. Decision Options

  • Without meaningful alternatives, there is no real decision.
  • Should require no more than about one-third of a page.
  • List the options concisely in bullet form.
  • Unless otherwise stated, treat the options as mutually exclusive.
  • Avoid creating hybrid options by combining alternatives that the case presents separately.

3. Decision Criteria

Decision criteria are the rules or standards by which the alternatives will be judged. They vary by case and must be stated explicitly; otherwise, the evidence presented later has no clear basis.

  • Criteria may range from technical measures, such as financial ratios, to broader strategic imperatives.
  • Select only the most important criteria—typically two to four.
  • Avoid criteria that are either overly specific or too vague.
ExampleAssessment
Firm must earn exactly 8.85% R.O.S.Too specific. What if R.O.S. is 8.75%?
Firm must improve its performance.Too general. What performance?
Firm must maintain or increase its R.O.S.Appropriate. Clear enough to evaluate alternatives without being artificially precise.

4. Proof of the Recommended Option

  • Explains why the recommended option best satisfies the decision criteria.
  • Addresses each criterion directly and supports the argument with relevant evidence from the case.

5. Critique of Rejected Options

  • Proving one option is attractive does not automatically invalidate the others.
  • Explain why each rejected option is less desirable when judged against the decision criteria.

6. Disadvantages of the Recommended Option

Every decision has weaknesses. A persuasive essay acknowledges them rather than pretending they do not exist.

  • Identify the most important weaknesses, risks, or tradeoffs associated with your recommendation.
  • Explain why those disadvantages are manageable and do not outweigh the recommendation’s strengths.

7. Action Plan

The action plan explains how the recommendation should be implemented.

  • Identify implementation goals.
  • Specify short-term and long-term actions needed to achieve those goals.
  • Identify important risks to monitor and possible countermeasures.
Course convention: For purposes of this course, present the action plan in concise bullet form. Detailed arguments or explanations for each action item are not required.
